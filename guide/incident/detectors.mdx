Detectors are checks that CloudThinker writes to cover the gaps in your existing alerts. Each Detector runs quietly in shadow beside your alert rules, proves itself on past and new incidents, and reaches triage only when a person makes it live.

## Why Detectors

- **Keep your alerting stack.** Detectors sit beside the alert rules you already run in tools such as Datadog, Grafana, and Prometheus. They do not replace them.
- **Find what your alerts miss.** An analysis compares your rules, recent signals, and past incidents to show the services where incidents went unnoticed.
- **Proof before paging.** Every proposed Detector comes with a test run and a backtest against past incidents, then runs in shadow, so nobody is paged until the check has earned it.
- **People decide what pages.** The agent proposes and tunes Detectors. Only a person can accept a Detector or make it live.
- **Graduate into your own rules.** A proven Detector can become a native rule in your alerts-as-code repository through a draft merge request.

## Turn it on

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect a monitoring source">
    Connect at least one monitoring platform in **Resolve → Integrations** so the agent can read its rules and metric history. See [Resolve integrations](/guide/incident/integrations).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the analysis">
    Open **Resolve → Detectors** and click **Analyze my monitoring**. This turns Detectors on and starts the first monitoring analysis right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the proposals">
    The analysis opens in the **Ask CloudThinker** drawer. When it finishes, review each proposed Detector on the **Needs you** tab, then click **Accept** or **Dismiss**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Success state:** an accepted Detector appears on the **Detectors** tab in the **Shadow** state.

A completed analysis can return no proposals. Its conversation explains what the agent reviewed and which access or evidence was missing. Click **Analyze again** after you fix it.

## How a Detector works

Each Detector moves through one loop.

1. **Audit** — An Alert Audit reviews your rules and flags the broken ones on the **Alert health** tab.
2. **Propose** — The agent proposes a Detector for a gap, with a reason, a test run, and a backtest.
3. **Watch in shadow** — An accepted Detector runs on a schedule. Its fires appear in Pulse with a Shadow badge and group with your own alerts, but they never start triage or page anyone.
4. **Learn** — A weekly review labels fires as real or noise, tightens noisy Detectors, retires the ones that stay noisy, and proposes new ones.
5. **Prove** — The weekly scorecard shows how many incidents each Detector caught first and how much earlier.
6. **Promote** — A proven Detector becomes a rule in your own alerting tool.

The checks themselves run no AI model. The agent runs that write, audit, and review Detectors use credits like any other agent run.

## Detector states

| State | What happens |
|---|---|
| Proposed | Waits for a person to accept or dismiss it. A dismissed proposal is deleted. |
| Shadow | Runs and records fires beside your alerts. Fires never start triage. |
| Live | Fires go to triage like any other alert, and triage decides whether anyone is paged. |
| Off | Paused. It does not run until someone clicks **Resume**. |
| Retired | Stops checking for good. Its history is kept. |

From the Detector detail panel you can **Make live**, move it **Back to shadow**, **Pause**, **Resume**, **Tune**, **Run backtest**, **Promote to rule**, or **Retire**. **Make live** and **Retire** ask you to confirm first.

### Who can change a state

| Action | A person | The agent, in a chat you started | The agent, in a scheduled run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propose | — | Yes | Yes |
| Accept, dismiss, make live | Yes | Only when you ask in that chat | No |
| Back to shadow, pause, retire | Yes | Yes | Yes, and the owner gets a notice |
| Tighten a check | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Loosen a check | Yes | Yes | Yes, and the owner gets a notice |

The agent acts with your own permissions in a chat, so it cannot do anything the page would refuse you. Members with read-only access see Detectors and their fires but cannot act on them.

## The Detectors page

**Resolve → Detectors** has four tabs. Each answers one question.

| Tab | Question it answers | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| Needs you | What waits on me? | Failing checks, new proposals, fires to label, and Detectors **Ready for live** |
| Detectors | What runs now? | Every Detector, with filters for state, service, and platform |
| Coverage gaps | Where do my alerts miss? | Services with a missed incident and no Detector, gaps first |
| Alert health | Which rules are broken? | The latest Alert Audit findings: **Noisy**, **Rarely useful**, and **Can't fire** |

Use **Ask CloudThinker** in the page header to ask a question, such as which rules to fix first, or to tell the agent what to watch.

```text
Where do my alerts miss incidents?
Watch checkout p95 and tell me when it stays above 800 ms
```

### Read a Detector's results

Open a Detector to see its scorecard, backtest, and fires.

- **Caught first** — the Detector fired before every one of your alerts and before the incident was created. Lead time is how much earlier it fired.
- **Missed** — no alert fired before the incident was created.
- **Fire labels** — each fire shows **Matched**, **False alarm**, **Suppressed**, or **Unmatched**. Click **Real** or **Noise** to label an unmatched fire. Only a person or the weekly review adds these labels.
- **Backtest** — replays the check over the last 30 days by default, up to 90 days. A fire counts as a catch when it lands between 60 minutes before impact and the moment the incident was created.

Results count CloudThinker incidents only. Incidents closed as false alarms do not count. Below three incidents in the window, the page shows counts instead of percentages.

A check that fails three times in a row is repaired by the agent or paused. If someone edits a check's script outside Resolve, the Detector shows **Changed outside Resolve** and its backtest is marked out of date.

## Promote a Detector to your own rule

Click **Promote to rule** on a proven Detector. Where the draft goes depends on the **Alert rule changes** setting:

- **With a rule repository** — Resolve opens a draft merge request with the native rule in the repository that holds your alert rules. A person reviews and merges it.
- **Without one** — Promote gives you the rule text. Click **Copy rule text** and add it in your alerting tool yourself.

To set the repository, turn on the **Propose alert rule changes** outcome for GitLab, GitHub, Bitbucket, or Azure DevOps in [Resolve integrations](/guide/incident/integrations). Resolve never changes a rule in your alerting tool directly.

## Settings

Configure Detectors in the **Detectors** section of [Resolve settings](/guide/incident/settings). Each control saves as soon as you change it.

| Setting | What it controls |
|---|---|
| Detectors | Turns every check and the weekly review on or off. Turning it off keeps your Detectors, fires, and audits. |
| Weekly review | The day and hour of the weekly review that labels fires, tunes Detectors, and sends the scorecard. The default is Monday. |
| Ignored services | Services that Detectors never audit, watch, or count as gaps. Use `*` as a wildcard, for example `*-staging`. Up to 50 entries. |
| Sources | The monitoring tools Detectors audit and write checks on. A source you turn off still counts in coverage. |
| Alert rule changes | The repository where promoted rules go as draft merge requests. |

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Active Detectors (Shadow and Live) per workspace | 50 |
| Open proposals per workspace | 5 |
| Fires per workspace per 24 hours | 200. Later fires are skipped and show **Daily fire budget used**. |
| Backtest window | 30 days by default, 90 days at most |
