Resolve settings decide whether CloudThinker investigates incidents on its own and where it reports progress. The two switches that control investigation and reporting are **On duty**, in the header of **Resolve → Investigation**, and **Live progress updates**, on **Resolve → Settings**. Both are workspace-wide, like **Enable On-call** on **Resolve → On-call**.

## Prerequisites

- The workspace **Admin** role to change any setting. Developers and Viewers can see the values but can't change them. See [Roles](/guide/security/roles).
- Resolve set up with at least one alert source. See [Connect Pulse sources](/guide/pulse/setup).

## Where each setting lives

Resolve spreads its controls across a few pages. Use this table to find the one you need.

| Setting | Where | Default | What it controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On duty** | **Resolve → Investigation**, page header | On | Whether CloudThinker triages and investigates on its own. See [What starts an investigation](#what-starts-an-investigation). |
| **Live progress updates** | **Resolve → Settings → Behaviour** | On | Whether progress and SITREP updates post to the incident's channels |
| **Auto-investigate** | Each webhook, under **Resolve → Integrations** | On | Whether that webhook's clusters go to triage and investigation on their own |
| **Only from a minimum severity** | Each webhook | Off | The lowest signal severity that starts triage on its own |
| **Send directly to CloudThinker** | Each webhook | Off | Whether alerts skip Pulse filtering and grouping. Use only for low-volume sources where every event matters. |
| **Enable On-call** | **Resolve → On-call** | Off | Whether a qualifying incident opens an Incident Room with your responders. See [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall). |
| **Policies** | **Resolve → Settings**, only where enabled | Not shown | The effect of each command from an uploaded runbook. See [Runbooks](/guide/incident/runbooks#control-command-effects). |

If you don't see **Policies**, it's off for your deployment. Email [support@cloudthinker.io](mailto:support@cloudthinker.io) to ask about it. Without it, workspace [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview) decide.

Every switch saves as soon as you flip it. There's no **Save** button.

## What starts an investigation

This table is the one place Resolve's start rules are written down. Other Resolve pages link here.

**Triage** is CloudThinker's first look at a cluster. It attaches the cluster to an open incident, reopens a closed incident when the same fault comes back, or opens a new one, and then investigates it (root-cause analysis, or RCA). A **signal** is an event that survived noise suppression, and a **cluster** is a group of related signals that Pulse treats as one problem. See [Pulse](/guide/pulse/overview).

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| **On duty**, webhook with **Auto-investigate** on | A cluster that holds an actionable signal goes to triage on its own. Triage opens or reopens an incident and investigates it. |
| **On duty**, webhook with **Only from a minimum severity** set | The same, but only signals at or above the floor start triage. Signals below it still group into clusters, and if their cluster already belongs to an open incident, they're added to that incident. |
| **On duty**, webhook with **Auto-investigate** off | Alerts still land in Pulse and group into clusters. Nothing starts on its own until a person clicks **Escalate** and then **Send to triage**. |
| **On duty**, webhook with **Send directly to CloudThinker** | Each event skips Pulse filtering and grouping and gets its own triage run. |
| **On duty**, polled AWS services and Slack or Teams channels | These sources have no **Auto-investigate** or severity option. Their actionable clusters always go to triage. |
| **Paused**, any source | Pulse keeps detecting and grouping alerts, and an actionable cluster is still filed as an incident with status **Open**. CloudThinker doesn't triage, investigate, or page responders on its own. Each incident waits until someone clicks **Investigate**. |
| A person clicks **Escalate**, then **Send to triage**, on a cluster | Triage runs right away, whichever way **On duty** is set. |
| A person clicks **Investigate** on an incident (Admins only) | The investigation starts right away, whichever way **On duty** is set. |
| A person [logs an incident](/guide/incident/manual-logging) | The investigation starts the moment the form is submitted, even while **Paused**. Nobody needs to click **Investigate**. |
| The same fault had an automatic investigation in the last 24 hours | CloudThinker starts at most one automatic investigation per fault in 24 hours. A person can still start one. |

**Auto-investigate** and **Only from a minimum severity** are set on each webhook under **Resolve → Integrations**. See [Webhook options](/guide/pulse/setup#webhook-options-that-change-behavior).

## Pause automatic investigation

**On duty** is CloudThinker's autonomy switch. It isn't your team's on-call rotation. Pause it during a planned maintenance window, or while you tune a noisy source. [What starts an investigation](#what-starts-an-investigation) shows what still runs while it's paused.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the investigation queue">
    Go to **Resolve → Investigation**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flip the switch">
    In the page header, turn off the **On duty** switch.

    **Success state:** the label next to the switch reads **Paused**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Work by hand while paused">
    On **Resolve → Pulse**, click **Escalate** on a cluster and then **Send to triage**, or click **Investigate** on an incident in **Resolve → Investigation**. Both work the same whether CloudThinker is on duty or paused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it back on">
    Flip the switch back. The label reads **On duty** again, and new actionable clusters are triaged on their own from that point. Incidents filed while paused stay **Open** until someone clicks **Investigate** on them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
There's no timer or auto-resume. **On duty** stays paused until someone turns it back on, so check the header after every maintenance window. The header doesn't show who paused it or when. Each change is recorded in [Audit Logs](/guide/security/audit-logs): filter **Category** to **Incident** and look for the raw action `incidents.settings_updated`. The entry shows who made the change, when, and which setting changed with its new value (not the old one). The same event covers **Live progress updates** changes.
</Tip>

## Choose where progress appears

While CloudThinker investigates, it writes progress notes and SITREPs (short situation reports: what's known, what's being checked, what's next).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open settings">
    Go to **Resolve → Settings**. The **Behaviour** section is at the top.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Live progress updates">
    Leave **Live progress updates** on to post each update to the incident's channels as it's written. Turn it off to keep updates on the incident's **Investigation timeline** only.

    **Success state:** the change applies right away, including to investigations already running: their next update posts (or stops posting) to their channels. To check it, watch the next update on an incident that already has an active war room or Incident Room.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The channels are the ones attached to the incident's response: its Slack war room, its Incident Room, and any Teams incident channel or Google Chat incident space. Updates post only while the incident has an active response. [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall) explains how a war room and Incident Room are attached.

Turn it off when a busy incident floods a shared channel. Nothing is lost: every update stays on the incident timeline.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The switches are greyed out">
    **Cause:** you don't have the workspace Admin role. The settings page says "You can view these settings, but only editors can change them." There, "editors" means workspace Admins. **Fix:** ask a workspace Admin to change the setting or your role.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="On duty is on, but an alert wasn't investigated">
    **Cause:** that webhook has **Auto-investigate** off, **Only from a minimum severity** is set above the alert's severity, or the same fault already had an automatic investigation in the last 24 hours. **Check:** open the webhook under **Resolve → Integrations**, and see [What starts an investigation](#what-starts-an-investigation). **Fix:** adjust the option, then click **Escalate** and **Send to triage** on the waiting cluster in **Resolve → Pulse**. More causes are in [Resolve troubleshooting](/guide/incident/troubleshooting).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
