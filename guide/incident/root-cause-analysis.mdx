Root-cause analysis (RCA) is the step where CloudThinker works out why an incident happened. It reads the incident, the alerts behind it, and your connected systems, tests possible explanations, and hands you a written verdict, a diagram of how the failure unfolded, and a list of suggested fixes. You review it and decide what happens next.

RCA is the Analyze stage of [Resolve](/guide/incident/overview). During analysis CloudThinker can hand focused checks to temporary subagents (short-lived helper agents) that read logs, metrics, traces, recent changes, and dependencies. Investigation reads your systems. Any action that would change something goes through a [runbook](/guide/incident/runbooks) and your [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview) settings.

## Before you start

- At least one [connection](/guide/connections/overview) to the systems involved, such as your cloud account, Kubernetes cluster, or monitoring tool. RCA can only check what it can read.
- The workspace **Admin** role to start investigations and settle suggestions. Developers and Viewers can read results. See [Roles](/guide/security/roles).

## How an investigation starts

| Incident came from | When RCA starts |
|---|---|
| [Pulse](/guide/pulse/overview) (an alert source) | Automatically, when **On Duty** (automatic investigation) is on and the incident qualifies. See [Resolve settings](/guide/incident/settings). |
| [Log Incident](/guide/incident/manual-logging) (a person) | Right away, as soon as you click **Log Incident**. |
| Anything else waiting in **Not started** | When you click **Investigate**. |

## Run an investigation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the incident">
    Go to **Resolve → Investigation** and select the incident from the queue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start RCA if it hasn't started">
    If the incident has never been investigated, click **Investigate** in the incident header.

    **Success state:** the incident moves to **AI is handling** and a **Watch live** button appears in the header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow along (optional)">
    Click **Watch live** to open the conversation where CloudThinker is working. You'll see each check it runs as it runs. The **Investigation timeline** on the incident also updates live.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the verdict">
    When analysis finishes, the incident moves to **Needs your decision** with the reason **Cause found**, or another outcome from the table below. Read the verdict, then settle the suggestions (next sections).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The investigation queue

**Resolve → Investigation** groups incidents by what they need from you:

| Group | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **AI is handling** | An investigation is queued or running |
| **Needs your decision** | A verdict is ready, the investigation paused for input, or the checks were inconclusive |
| **Not started** | No investigation has run yet. Click **Investigate** to start one. |
| **Resolved** | Closed as **Resolved** or **Auto-resolved** |
| **Dismissed** | Closed as **False Alarm**, or judged as needing no action |

## Read the result

The incident page puts the answer first and the supporting material below it.

| Section | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Current assessment** | The written verdict, an evidence label (below), and how long it took to identify the root cause. Shows **Still investigating** while a session is running. |
| **How it happened** | A causal graph from the first failure to the visible impact |
| Severity suggestion | Appears when CloudThinker thinks the severity is wrong. Click **Change to** the suggested level, or **Keep** the current one. |
| **Remediation** | Suggested fixes, each with a priority |
| **Full analysis** | The complete written analysis |
| **Investigation timeline** | Every step, newest first, including each explanation tested and whether it was confirmed or ruled out |
| **Affected services** | Services and dependencies involved |
| **Duplicate webhooks** | Repeat deliveries of the same alert, when there were any |

### Evidence labels

The label next to **Current assessment** says how well the root cause is backed:

| Label | Meaning | How to treat it |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | Confirmed against data from your systems | Safe to act on after a quick review |
| **Inferred** | Reasoned from the evidence, not directly confirmed | Check the timeline before acting |
| **Cause unproven** | The verdict rests on an assumption | Treat it as a lead. Add access or context and investigate again. |

### Settle each remediation suggestion

Hover a suggestion in **Remediation**, open its **More remediation actions** menu, and choose:

| Action | Use it when | Add a note about |
|---|---|---|
| **Mark as Done** | Your team did the work | What you actually did |
| **Dismiss** | It doesn't apply or isn't worth doing | Why |

The note is optional and stays on the suggestion for anyone reviewing the incident. A settled suggestion keeps its state; a later investigation session doesn't overwrite it.

## Investigation outcomes

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Identified** | A root cause was found |
| **On Hold** | Paused, waiting for information, access, or a decision from a person. The incident sits in **Needs your decision** until someone supplies it. |
| **Not Found** | The available evidence didn't support a root cause. These incidents drop out of the queue. |
| **False Alarm** | The alert wasn't a real problem |
| **Resolved** | Fixed and closed with a person involved |
| **Auto-resolved** | Fixed and closed by the agent with nobody involved |

**Acknowledged** is not an outcome: it means a responder owns the incident but analysis hasn't started.

## Re-investigation

An incident has one investigation record. When a new session runs, whether a person or the system started it, its steps are added to the same **Investigation timeline** under a session marker. If the conclusion changes, the timeline records a root-cause revision instead of creating a second report. Earlier sessions stay available under **More actions → Earlier sessions**. [Resolve settings](/guide/incident/settings) controls how readily new evidence triggers a new session.

## Correlated incidents

When several alerts share a likely cause, Resolve groups them under one parent incident and runs a single RCA there. Opening a grouped alert shows a **Part of a Correlated Group** card:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **View Parent Analysis** | Opens the parent incident that owns the investigation |
| **Promote to Standalone** | Detaches the alert into its own incident. After you confirm, choose whether to start RCA on it now or leave it for later. Admin only. |

## Share or export the result

From the incident's **More actions** menu:

- **Download analysis PDF** saves the analysis as a PDF.
- **Open AI Conversation** opens the full investigation conversation, where you can ask follow-up questions.
- **View source context** shows the alerts and Pulse data the incident came from.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| No **Investigate** button | The incident already has an investigation, a session is running, or it's part of a correlated group (use **View Parent Analysis**). It also needs the Admin role. |
| Pulse incidents stay in **Not started** | **On Duty** is off, or the incident didn't qualify. Check [Resolve settings](/guide/incident/settings). |
| Verdict is **Cause unproven** or **Not Found** | RCA couldn't read enough. Add the missing [connection](/guide/connections/overview), add context in the conversation, and investigate again. |
| Stuck in **On Hold** | Open the conversation with **Open AI Conversation** and answer what CloudThinker asked for. |
