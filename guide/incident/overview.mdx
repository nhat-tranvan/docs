**Finds the root cause. In minutes.**

Resolve is the Frontier Investigation Agent for production incidents. It takes the alerts your tools already send, drops the noise, investigates what's left to a root cause, and brings in people only when an incident needs them.

## How it works

Inside Resolve, an alert moves through four stages. They're Resolve's own steps of the platform loop described in [How CloudThinker works](/#how-cloudthinker-works) on the docs home page.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[1. Detect] --> B[2. Investigate]
    B --> C[3. Respond]
    C --> D[4. Remember]
    D -. "read by a later investigation" .-> B
```

| Stage | What happens | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Detect | [Pulse](/guide/pulse/overview), the detection layer, suppresses known noise and groups related alerts into clusters, so one outage is one item. | **Resolve → Pulse** |
| 2. Investigate | CloudThinker triages each actionable cluster, opens or reopens an incident, and runs a root-cause analysis (RCA) across your connected systems. | **Resolve → Investigation** |
| 3. Respond | When people are needed, [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall) opens an Incident Room for the responders. Every fix CloudThinker runs goes through [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview), so nothing changes without the policy you set. | Incident Room, Slack war room |
| 4. Remember | CloudThinker writes [incident memory](/guide/incident/incident-memory) that later investigations read at the start. | Inside the next RCA |

Not every incident reaches every stage. An investigation can end without a root cause, and a **Track only** incident pages no one unless a person raises it.

## Set up in this order

You need the workspace **Admin** role to change Resolve settings and integrations. See [Roles](/guide/security/roles).

1. **Connect an alert source.** Point a monitoring tool at Resolve, or try a test source first with the [quickstart](/guide/incident/quickstart). See [Connect Pulse sources](/guide/pulse/setup).
2. **Check On duty.** It's on in a new workspace, so CloudThinker investigates on its own. See [below](#choose-who-starts-investigations).
3. **Set up On-call.** Pick responders and, optionally, a Slack war room. See [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall).
4. **Review your Approvals policy.** It decides which fixes run on their own and which wait for a person. See [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview).

## Start from the Overview

Open **Resolve → Overview** to see what needs you now and what Resolve handled alone.

| Tile | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Needs your decision** | Incidents marked **Cause found** or **Paused**. Open one to review the cause and remediation suggestions, or answer what CloudThinker asked. |
| **Awaiting your approval** | Runbooks staged and blocked on an approval decision |
| **Critical & high open** | Unresolved incidents at the top two severities |
| **AI is handling** | Incidents being investigated right now, no action needed |

Each tile opens the matching slice of the Investigation queue. The page also shows the funnel from alerts to incidents. If a connected source stops sending, a banner says so, so a broken feed doesn't look like a quiet system.

| Funnel step | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Event** | One raw notification from a source, such as a CloudWatch alarm |
| **Signal** | An event that survived noise suppression |
| **Cluster** | Related signals Pulse treats as one problem |
| **Incident** | What triage, or a person, opens from a cluster. CloudThinker investigates it. |

[Pulse](/guide/pulse/overview) covers the first three in detail.

## Choose who starts investigations

The **On duty** switch sits in the header of **Resolve → Investigation**. It's CloudThinker's autonomy switch, not your human on-call rotation. It's on in a new workspace, and only a workspace Admin can change it.

| Switch | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **On duty** | CloudThinker detects, triages, and investigates on its own. |
| **Paused** | Pulse keeps detecting and incidents are filed as Open. RCA waits until someone clicks **Investigate**. |

Manual investigation always works, whichever way the switch is set.

## What you can do

| Job | What it covers | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Try it end to end | Send a test alert and follow it from Pulse to an incident | [Resolve quickstart](/guide/incident/quickstart) |
| Connect alert sources | Webhooks, cloud polling, Slack and Teams channels, and where Resolve posts updates | [Integrations](/guide/incident/integrations) |
| Work the alert feed | Lanes, clusters, Escalate, Attach to incident, snooze | [Pulse](/guide/pulse/overview) |
| Read a root cause | Verdict, confidence, evidence, timeline, remediation suggestions | [Investigation and RCA](/guide/incident/root-cause-analysis) |
| Bring people in | Responders, paging, the Incident Room, and the Slack war room | [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall) |
| Log something Pulse didn't see | A customer report or an operator hunch | [Log an incident](/guide/incident/manual-logging) |
| Govern fixes | Which actions run on their own and which wait for a person | [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview) |
| Add fix procedures | Your written procedures from Confluence, GitHub, GitLab, or Markdown, read during RCA (marked **Legacy** in the sidebar) | [Runbooks](/guide/incident/runbooks) |
| Measure results | Trends, accuracy, and per-service reliability | [Insights](/guide/incident/insights) |
| Tune behavior | Live progress updates and response policies | [Settings](/guide/incident/settings) |

Coming soon: [Detectors](/guide/incident/detectors), checks CloudThinker writes from your incidents to catch what alerts miss.
