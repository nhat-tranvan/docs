**Resolve → Insights** shows how Resolve, CloudThinker's incident response module, performed over a time window you pick, and whether you can trust its diagnoses. Use it to decide what to fix next: a noisy alert source, a service with slow recovery, or a diagnosis your team keeps correcting.

## Before you start

- A workspace with some [Resolve](/guide/incident/overview) incidents, from alerts or [logged by hand](/guide/incident/manual-logging). While the workspace has no incidents and nobody has finished Resolve setup, every Resolve page, Insights included, shows a setup screen instead. Click **Connect monitoring**, or an Admin can click **Skip for now** to see the tabs, empty until incidents arrive.
- Any workspace role. Admin, Developer, and Viewer can all open Insights. See [Roles](/guide/security/roles).
- For the accuracy numbers: workspace Admins marking diagnoses right or wrong (see [Mark a diagnosis](#mark-a-diagnosis-right-or-wrong)).

## Open Insights and pick a window

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the page">
    In the sidebar, open **Resolve → Insights**, under **Live Operations**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a time window">
    Use the selector in the page header. Options run from **Last 1 hour** to **Last 30 days**, and the default is **Last 7 days**. The window stays the same when you switch tabs.

    Each tab filters on a different time:

    | Tab or card | Window filters on |
    |---|---|
    | **Reliability**, **Accuracy and trust** | When Resolve recorded the incident, not when it resolved or was marked. An incident recorded last week and resolved today stays in last week's numbers. |
    | **MTTR**, **Auto-resolved** | Same as above, but unresolved incidents are left out until they close, so the last few days' numbers keep moving. |
    | **Signal quality** | Differs per card. See [Pulse analytics](/guide/pulse/analytics#what-each-metric-means). |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a tab">
    Choose **Reliability**, **Accuracy and trust**, or **Signal quality**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Success state:** the tab fills with cards for the window you chose. A card that says something like "No incident resolved in this period" means there's no data yet, not an error.

Numbers are computed from live data when the tab loads, so reload the page to refresh them. The page has no export button.

## Choose the right tab

| Tab | Question it answers | Start here when |
|---|---|---|
| **Reliability** | How fast does Resolve get from alert to fix, and which services hurt most? | Your on-call lead asks why last week felt slow. |
| **Accuracy and trust** | How often did people confirm Resolve's root cause was correct? | You're deciding how much to rely on automatic investigation. |
| **Signal quality** | How much alert noise does Signals absorb, and which sources make the most? | Responders complain about alert volume. |

## Read the Reliability tab

| Card | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **MTTR** | Mean time to resolve: the average time from when the incident occurred (its **Occurred At** time, or when it was recorded if that's missing) to when it was resolved, across resolved incidents. It's a mean, so one long incident pulls it up. Use it to spot outliers. |
| **Alert → root cause** | Median time from the incident starting to a root cause being found. It covers detection, correlation, and analysis together, not only how long the agent ran. Because it's a median, one bad incident barely moves it, so it's the better number for trend reporting. |
| **Auto-resolved** | Share of resolved incidents CloudThinker closed with no human touch. This is the incident status **Auto-resolved**, not the 12-hour cluster auto-close in Signals. |
| **Event → incident reduction** | Raw events that didn't become an incident, out of all raw events: (raw events − incidents) ÷ raw events. Raw events is the same count as **Signals ingested** on [Signal quality](/guide/pulse/analytics#what-each-metric-means): it includes dropped duplicates and leaves out deliveries over a webhook's rate limit. The help icon shows the funnel: raw events → signals that weren't suppressed → clusters → incidents. |
| **Where the time goes** | Average time spent in each stage. See [Stages](#stages-in-where-the-time-goes). |
| **MTTR over time** | The most recently resolved incidents in the window, with how long each took to resolve. |
| **Recurrence trend** | Share of incidents that came back within 72 hours of a matching incident resolving. Two incidents match when they share a dedup key, an identifier CloudThinker sets on incidents created from [webhook alerts](/guide/incident/webhook-integrations/overview). Manually logged incidents have none, so they aren't counted. |
| **Reliability by service** | Per service: **Incidents**, **Latest**, and **Time to resolve**. |

### Stages in Where the time goes

| Stage | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **First signal → root cause** | Detecting the incident, correlating its alerts into a cluster, and finding the root cause. Manually logged incidents have no cluster, so the correlate part is left out for them. |
| **Root cause → fix** | Waiting for a person to approve or apply the fix. This is mostly approval wait time, not how long the fix took. If it's long, check pending requests in [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview). |
| **Validate** | From the last fix finishing to the incident closing |

When total response time goes up, check this card first. A slow **First signal → root cause** stage can point at your alert sources as well as root cause analysis (RCA).

## Read the Accuracy and trust tab

Every number on this tab comes from people on your team marking a diagnosis as right or wrong. Nothing comes from the agent scoring itself.

| Card | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Root-cause accuracy** | Diagnoses marked accurate, out of all reviewed diagnoses. Reviewed means someone marked it, either way. |
| **Fix acceptance** | Runbook executions a person approved, out of executions that needed approval. Pending and rejected requests both count as needing approval and not accepted, so a backlog of pending approvals lowers the rate. Runbooks that ran without approval aren't counted. |
| **False-positive rate** | Diagnoses marked wrong with the reason **False positive**, out of all reviewed diagnoses (the same set as **Root-cause accuracy**). |
| **Your review coverage** | Despite the name, a team number: resolved incidents anyone in the workspace reviewed, out of all resolved incidents. |
| **When Resolve was off, why?** | The reasons people gave when they marked a diagnosis wrong. |
| **Review more incidents** | How many resolved incidents are still unmarked. |
| **Accuracy by service** | **Reviewed**, **Accurate**, and **Rate** per service. Services come from each incident's **Affected services** list, which the investigation fills in. |

Use the reasons to decide what to change:

| Reason | What it usually points at |
|---|---|
| **Wrong root cause** | Missing evidence. Connect the logs, metrics, or traces the investigation needed. |
| **Wrong service** | Missing dependency data. Connect the systems that show how services call each other, such as your Kubernetes cluster or tracing tool, so the investigation can follow the path. See [Connections](/guide/connections/overview). |
| **Fix wouldn't work** | Remediation quality. Add or update a [runbook](/guide/incident/runbooks). |
| **False positive** | A noisy alert. Tune it at the source or in [Signals](/guide/pulse/clusters). |
| **Something else** | No pattern to act on from this tab. Read the note left with the mark: the incident shows it, and **Corrections Resolve reads** lists it. |

<Warning>
Before you act on **Root-cause accuracy**:

- Check **Your review coverage**. With only a handful of reviewed incidents, one verdict swings the rate a lot.
- Each Admin's mark counts as a separate review. Two Admins marking the same incident count twice in **Root-cause accuracy**, while **Your review coverage** counts the incident once.
- Read the underlying incidents before you change a connection, runbook, or process.
</Warning>

### Mark a diagnosis right or wrong

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the incident">
    Go to **Resolve → Issues** and open the incident. It doesn't have to be resolved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer the question">
    Below the analysis, under **Was Resolve's diagnosis right?**, click the thumbs-up (**Accurate**) or thumbs-down (**Inaccurate**) button. When the verdict names a root cause, the buttons read **Confirm cause** and **Not the cause**. The question appears as soon as the incident has a verdict. Only Developers and Admins can answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what was wrong">
    If you click **Inaccurate** or **Not the cause**, pick the closest reason under **What was wrong?** and add an optional note under **What was the actual cause?**. Resolve reads that note on the next alert of the same fault. The mark only saves once you pick a reason.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Success state:** the incident shows **Marked accurate** (or **Cause confirmed**) or **Marked wrong**, and the verdict counts on the **Accuracy and trust** tab. Any mark counts toward **Root-cause accuracy** and **False-positive rate**. Only marks on resolved incidents count toward **Your review coverage**. Each person has their own mark on an incident and can change it later.

## Read the Signal quality tab

This tab shows how much alert noise [Signals](/guide/pulse/overview) absorbs and which sources send the most. Every card and column is defined in [Pulse analytics](/guide/pulse/analytics). Its **Noise suppressed** card uses a different denominator from **Event → incident reduction** on Reliability, so don't compare the two.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Every tab is empty">
    **Cause:** no incidents fall inside the window. **Check:** widen the selector to **Last 30 days**. **Fix:** if it's still empty, confirm alerts are arriving on **Resolve → Signals**, then follow [Pulse setup](/guide/pulse/setup).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Accuracy shows no percentage">
    **Cause:** nobody has marked a diagnosis in this window. **Fix:** mark a few resolved incidents under **Was Resolve's diagnosis right?**. The **Review more incidents** card shows how many are waiting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A service is missing from Accuracy by service">
    **Cause:** services on this table come from each incident's **Affected services** list. An incident counts only when it was marked and its investigation named the service. **Check:** open a marked incident for that service and look at **Affected services**. **Fix:** if it's empty, the investigation couldn't place the service. Add the connection it needed, so later investigations can.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A tab shows a load error">
    Click **Retry**. If it keeps failing, see [Resolve troubleshooting](/guide/incident/troubleshooting).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
