Approvers are the people who may answer an approval card, the prompt an agent shows when a write must wait for a person. **Activity** shows what each [Rule](/guide/approvals/rules) decided and who approved, so you can audit it later.

## Prerequisites

- The workspace Admin role to change approvers or the two-person rule. Developers and Viewers can view them.
- A workspace owner or admin to turn break-glass on or off. A workspace owner has every Admin right in Approvals; see [who can change Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview#who-can-change-approvals).

## Default approvers

**Default approvers** is a built-in group that answers every ask no Rule sends to another group. You cannot rename or delete it.

When it has no members, every Developer or above can approve. Viewers can never approve, so you cannot add them to any group.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Approvers tab">
    Open **Approvals** in the sidebar, then the **Approvers** tab. **Default approvers** is the first row.

    <Frame caption="Approvers: the Default approvers group, New group, and the two-person and break-glass switches">
      <img src="/images/v2/approvals-approvers.webp" alt="Approvers tab with the Default approvers row, the New group button, and the two-person and break-glass switches" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add members">
    Click **Edit** on **Default approvers**, add the people who should answer most asks under **Members**, then click **Save group**.

    **Success state:** the row lists those people instead of "No one listed, so every Developer or above can approve."
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Send one kind of write to a group

An approver group answers the asks of the **Ask first** Rules that name it, instead of Default approvers. Use one when a specific team owns a system, such as DBAs for database deletes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the group">
    On the **Approvers** tab, click **New group**. Enter a **Group name**, such as `On-call SRE`, add **Members**, then click **Create group**. Members are people already in this workspace; Viewers aren't listed. A policy file names them by email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the group in a Rule">
    Open an **Ask first** Rule on the **Rules** tab. Under **Who approves this?**, pick the group and save.

    **Success state:** the group row on the **Approvers** tab says "Used by 1 Rule".
  </Step>
</Steps>

When one write matches more than one Rule, these people may approve:

| Matched Rules | Who may approve |
|---------------|-----------------|
| No Rule names a group | Default approvers |
| One Rule names a group | Members of that group |
| Rules name different groups | People who are in every named group |
| No one is left | Workspace owners and admins |

For example, if one write matches a Rule that names `DBA` and another that names `SRE`, only people in both groups can approve. If no one is in both, workspace owners and admins approve.

If no one at all can approve, the card says "No one else can approve this. Ask a workspace owner to add an approver." You cannot delete a group while a Rule names it.

## Require a second person

The two-person rule stops the person who started a run from approving their own write. One other approver is enough; it is not a vote.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn it on">
    On the **Approvers** tab, turn on **The person who started a run cannot approve it**. You need at least 2 approvers first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick what it applies to">
    Under **Applies to**, pick one:

    | Applies to | Who must approve |
    |------------|------------------|
    | **Destructive actions** | Another approver for deletes, remote shell sessions such as `ssh`, and any call CloudThinker can't identify (see [access badges](/guide/approvals/rules#what-a-rule-contains)) |
    | **All writes** | Another approver for every write |

    **Success state:** the next time you start a run that needs approval, your card shows no **Approve** button and names who else can approve.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This covers every ask, not only asks from a Rule. To require two people for one kind of write only, select **Needs 2 different people** in that Rule instead.

<Warning>
With **All writes**, an on-call engineer working alone can't approve their own fixes. Make sure a second approver is reachable at all hours, or pick **Destructive actions**.
</Warning>

## Break-glass

Break-glass lets a workspace owner or admin run one call that a **Deny** Rule refused, for a real emergency. It is off by default.

1. An owner or admin turns on **Let owners and admins break glass** on the **Approvers** tab.
2. When a **Deny** Rule refuses a call in a chat with a person in it, an owner or admin clicks **Break glass…** on the card. Only a Deny Rule refusal qualifies. If a **Refuse** mode refused the call, switch the chat to an **Ask me** mode instead.
3. They give a reason of 20 characters or more within 15 minutes of the ask. The call runs once.

Workspace owners and admins get an in-app notification with the reason and a link to the chat. It isn't one of the routable events in [Notifications](/guide/notifications). The reason also stays in the [audit log](/guide/security/audit-logs). The agent cannot ask for break-glass. With the two-person rule on, the person who started the run cannot break glass on it either.

<Warning>
Break-glass is a security event. It never runs an **Always blocked** call or a call refused by a Rule marked **Never allow break-glass**.
</Warning>

## Check Activity

Open **Approvals → Activity** to audit your Rules. Pick a date range of up to 366 days. The table has one row per Rule for that period, plus a **No Rule matched** row for writes the Approval mode decided, such as AI judgment or a Manual ask.

| Column | What it shows |
|--------|---------------|
| **Rule**, **Source**, **Accepted by** | What the Rule is, where it came from, and who accepted it |
| **Allowed**, **Asked first**, **Refused** | How many calls the Rule decided each way |
| **Approved**, **Declined**, **Approvers** | How people answered its asks, and who answered |
| **Break-glass** | How many times someone broke glass on it |

Filter by **Source**, **Effect**, or **Result**, or use **Search Rules or people**. Click **Export CSV** to download every Rule in the period, for example for a quarterly access review.

Call counts go back 90 days, because CloudThinker keeps each decision record for 90 days. Times in the table are UTC. The **Overview** tab shows the same totals for the last 7 days: calls that ran, asked a person, or were refused.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I can't turn on the two-person rule">
    The switch needs at least 2 people who can approve. Add another approver to Default approvers or make sure a second Developer or Admin is in the workspace, then try again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Someone can't see the Approve button">
    They may be a Viewer, they may have started the run while the two-person rule is on, or the matched Rule sends the ask to a group they are not in. The card names who else can approve.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Break glass… does not show on a refused card">
    Break-glass must be on, you must be a workspace owner or admin, and a workspace **Deny** Rule must have refused the call in a chat with a person in it. It never shows for **Always blocked** calls or Rules marked **Never allow break-glass**, and it ends 15 minutes after the ask.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Activity says No decisions in this period">
    Pick a longer date range. If it stays empty, Rules may still be in preview in this workspace, so they record nothing as a decision yet.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
