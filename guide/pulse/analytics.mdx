Pulse analytics tells you whether your alert pipeline is doing useful work. In one tab you see how many raw events came in, how many Pulse held back as noise, how many reached an incident or were resolved, and which sources send the most volume.

A few terms used on this page:

- **Signal**: one raw event Pulse received, such as a single Datadog alert or CloudWatch alarm. Pulse can mark a Signal as suppressed (noise). The rest are visible Signals.
- **Cluster**: a group of related Signals that Pulse believes describe the same problem.
- **Reached an outcome**: a Signal whose Cluster was either linked to an incident or resolved. **Correlation yield** and the **Clusters routed** column both count this (includes auto-closed, see [Auto-resolved Clusters](#auto-resolved-clusters)).

See [Clusters](/guide/pulse/clusters) for the full lifecycle.

## Open Pulse analysis

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Insights">
    In the sidebar, open **Resolve → Insights**, under **Analytics**. The workspace Admin, Developer, and Viewer roles can all open it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the Pulse tab">
    Click the **Pulse analysis** tab. The other tabs (**Overview**, **Accuracy & trust**, **Runbook analytics**) are covered in [Resolve insights](/guide/incident/insights).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the time window">
    Use the window selector at the top right. Options run from **Last 1 hour** to **Last 30 days**. The default is **Last 7 days**. Each card filters on a different time, listed in the **Window filters on** column below. A Cluster that formed 8 days ago and resolved yesterday is missing from **Cluster MTTR** in **Last 7 days**, but its Signals from the last 7 days still count in **Correlation yield**.

    **Success state:** four metric cards appear, followed by the **Top noisy sources** table. If no Signals arrived in the window, the table reads "No signals ingested in this period".
  </Step>
</Steps>

Numbers are computed when the tab loads. Reload to refresh. There is no export.

## What each metric means

| Card | What it measures | How it's calculated | Window filters on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correlation yield** | How much of your alert volume reached an outcome | Signals that reached an outcome, divided by all stored Signals (visible plus suppressed). It counts Signals, not Clusters, and covers every source (not just the top 10). | Signal time |
| **Cluster MTTR** | How long problems stay open | Median time from a Cluster's first Signal to the moment it was marked resolved (includes auto-closed, see [Auto-resolved Clusters](#auto-resolved-clusters)). | When the Cluster first formed |
| **Signals ingested** | Raw volume | Every delivery Pulse received, including duplicates it dropped without storing. Deliveries over a webhook's rate limit aren't counted (see the warning below). | Hour the delivery arrived |
| **Noise suppressed** | How much Pulse filtered out | Suppressed Signals divided by stored Signals (visible plus suppressed). Dropped duplicates aren't stored, so they count in **Signals ingested** but not here. The subtitle shows visible Signals collapsing into Clusters, for example `1,240 signals → 38 clusters`. | Signal time |

Because of dropped duplicates, **Signals ingested** is usually larger than the Signal count behind the other cards.

**Cluster MTTR** is a median over Clusters. **Mean time to resolve** on the **Overview** tab is a mean over incidents. Expect them to differ.

<Warning>
Deliveries over a webhook's rate limit (1,000 per hour by default) are dropped silently, still return `200`, and appear nowhere on this tab. Resolve doesn't show a dropped count. If alerts from a burst are missing, compare the number your tool sent that hour with **Signals ingested**, then raise the webhook's limit or split the monitors. See [Limits and responses](/guide/incident/webhook-integrations/overview#limits-and-responses).
</Warning>

### Auto-resolved Clusters

A Cluster with no new Signal for 12 hours closes on its own and counts as resolved. That affects three numbers:

- **Correlation yield** and **Clusters routed** count it as reaching an outcome, even if nobody looked at it. A high yield means alerts settled into Clusters that ended, not that every alert mattered. Read it next to the **Routed** lane on **Resolve → Pulse**, which shows only Clusters linked to an incident.
- **Cluster MTTR** measures it to the auto-close time, not to its last Signal.

<Warning>
An untouched Cluster takes at least 12 hours to resolve, so it adds 12 hours or more to **Cluster MTTR**. If most Clusters close on their own, the median mostly reflects that timer. To make MTTR track real recovery, click **Resolve** on the Cluster panel when the problem ends. See [Action reference](/guide/pulse/clusters#action-reference).
</Warning>

<Note>
**Noise suppressed** is not the same number as **Noise reduced** on the **Overview** tab. Overview measures raw events collapsing into incidents. Pulse analysis measures suppressed Signals over all Signals seen. Compare each number only with itself over time.
</Note>

## Top noisy sources

The table ranks the 10 sources that sent the most Signals in the window.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Source** | The raw source name, such as `webhook.datadog` or a CloudTrail stream with account and region. Hover a long name to see all of it. |
| **Signals** | Stored Signals from that source in the window, visible plus suppressed. Dropped duplicates aren't counted. |
| **Suppressed** | Share of that source's Signals Pulse suppressed |
| **Clusters routed** | Despite the name, the share of that source's Signals whose Cluster was linked to an incident **or** resolved (includes auto-closed, see [Auto-resolved Clusters](#auto-resolved-clusters)). For Clusters linked to an incident only, use the **Routed** lane on **Resolve → Pulse**. |

One integration can show up as several rows. A single AWS connection, for example, can produce a separate stream per account and region.

## Read the results

Use the numbers to decide where to spend tuning time. There's no benchmark for a healthy percentage, because it depends on your sources. Compare each number with its own trend week over week.

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A source with high **Signals** and near 0% **Clusters routed** | It sends a lot and almost none of it reaches an incident or a resolution | Open **Resolve → Pulse** and pick that source in the source filter (it reads **Any source** until you choose). Tighten the alert at the source, or **Snooze** its repeating Signals. See [Noise suppression](/guide/pulse/clusters#noise-suppression) |
| Low **Correlation yield** overall | Most Signals never reach an incident or a resolution | Check whether the top sources are noise first. If they aren't, check that each webhook maps **Service Path**, which Pulse uses to group related alerts. See [Map the fields](/guide/incident/webhook-integrations/overview#map-the-fields). |
| **Cluster MTTR** rising week over week | Problems are staying open longer | Check ownership and routing in [On-call](/guide/incident/oncall), and look for Clusters nobody picked up |
| A sudden jump in **Noise suppressed** for one source | A new event pattern, or a snooze that now matches too much (see the checks below the table) | Open **Resolve → Pulse**, pick that source in the source filter, and open a few of its Signals. A Signal's detail panel says whether Pulse suppressed it and why. If a snooze is the cause, click **Unsnooze** on a matching Signal. See [Noise suppression](/guide/pulse/clusters#noise-suppression) |

Pulse suppresses Signals with four checks:

- Dedup of repeat deliveries
- A per-source rate limit
- A **Snooze** someone set
- Noise patterns it learned

Details for each are in [Noise suppression](/guide/pulse/clusters#noise-suppression).

For example, a row reading `webhook.datadog`, **Signals** 4,800, **Suppressed** 92%, **Clusters routed** 1% says Datadog sends a lot of repeats and almost none of it leads anywhere. Open a few of its Signals on **Resolve → Pulse** to find the monitors behind them, then raise their thresholds or turn them off in Datadog.

Treat these as pointers, not verdicts. Look at the actual Clusters and Signals before you change a source or a snooze.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Every card shows zero or is empty | No Signals arrived in the window. Widen the window, or confirm a source is sending in [Connect Pulse sources](/guide/pulse/setup). |
| **Cluster MTTR** is empty | No Clusters were resolved in the window. It fills in once Clusters resolve. |
| "Failed to Load Pulse Analysis" | Click **Retry**. If it keeps failing, reload the page. Still failing? Email [support@cloudthinker.io](mailto:support@cloudthinker.io) with your workspace name, the time window you picked, the time of the error, and the error text. |
| You see **Connect your first alert source** instead of **Insights** | The workspace has no incidents yet and nobody has finished Resolve setup. Click **Connect a source** (see [Connect Pulse sources](/guide/pulse/setup)), or an Admin can click **Skip for now**. |
