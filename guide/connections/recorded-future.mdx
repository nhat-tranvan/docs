Connect Recorded Future to let CloudThinker investigate threats and update notes and watchlists. The connection uses **Recorded Future OAuth authorization**.

## Prerequisites

- An account with access to the Recorded Future resources you intend to investigate.
- The **Admin** role in your CloudThinker workspace to add and manage the connection. See [Roles](/guide/security/roles).
- Permission to authorize this integration for the intended organization.

## Setup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Prepare access in Recorded Future">
    Use a Recorded Future account with access to the intelligence modules you need. CloudThinker opens the provider authorization flow when you connect; sign in and review the requested access before granting it.

    Follow the [vendor’s credential and authentication guidance](https://support.recordedfuture.com/hc/en-us/articles/52470642765587-Connecting-to-Recorded-Future-MCP) for your account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect in CloudThinker">
    Open **Settings → Workspace → Connections**, find **Recorded Future**, and click **Connect**. Sign in to the provider and review the consent screen. Authorize the intended organization, then return to CloudThinker.

    **Success state:** the connection shows **Connected**. Run the verification prompt below to check what the account can actually read.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the scope">
    Run the [verification prompt](#verify-the-connection). Compare the returned organization, projects, or resource names with the account you intended to connect. Keep a denied request visible as an access problem; do not interpret it as an empty result.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connection details

There are no API-key fields to fill in. The provider supplies OAuth credentials during authorization. To use another identity, disconnect this connection and authorize again with that identity.

An unavailable module is an entitlement gap, not an empty or healthy result. Reconnect with the intended account if the organization is wrong.

## Required permissions

The authorizing user’s organization, intelligence-module entitlements, and granted OAuth access determine what is available. Notes, watchlists, and other mutations require the corresponding provider permission and the workspace’s approval policy.

<Tip>
Start with read access to the smallest useful scope. Adding a connection does not grant permissions that the provider account lacks. For supported changes, [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview) decides whether CloudThinker may proceed; provider permissions still apply.
</Tip>

## Agent capabilities

| Workflow | What agents can do |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Confirm the connected organization and identity |
| Investigation | Investigate entities and intelligence cards |
| Investigation | Review alerts and attack-surface findings when entitled |
| Changes | Update notes or watchlists when authorized |

### Verify the connection

```text
Confirm the Recorded Future organization and investigate a threat entity I provide, citing current intelligence. Report any denied or unavailable checks separately.
```

Expect current resource names or a clear access error. No matching records is a valid outcome only when the request succeeded and its account, filters, and time window match your question.

### Example prompts

```text
Confirm the Recorded Future organization and investigate a threat entity I provide, citing current intelligence
Use this connection to inspect one resource from the results and explain its current state with supporting evidence
```

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Authentication fails">
    Complete the provider sign-in and consent flow again with the intended account. Check that your organization permits the integration and that the browser allowed its authorization window.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Connected, but some resources are missing">
    Compare the provider account’s access, selected organization, and investigation filters. A successful sign-in does not prove access to every resource. An unavailable module is an entitlement gap, not an empty or healthy result. Reconnect with the intended account if the organization is wrong.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Requests are denied or cannot reach the service">
    A permission denial needs a provider access change for the specific read, not a broader account by default. For a timeout or certificate error, confirm the HTTPS endpoint, network route, and trusted CA with the service administrator. For throttling, narrow the investigation and retry after the provider’s indicated wait.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Security

<ConnectionSecurity />

- Keep the connection restricted to the account and resources intended for this workspace.
- Revoke the provider grant and reconnect if the authorizing account or organization changes.
