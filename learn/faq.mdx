## Getting started

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is CloudThinker?">
    CloudThinker builds Frontier Investigation Agents. These AI [agents](/guide/agents/overview) connect to your cloud, code, and tools, work out the root cause of incidents, code risks, cost waste, and security gaps, and propose a fix for your engineers to approve.

    Four products do the investigating. You can adopt them one at a time:

    | Product | What it does |
    |---|---|
    | [Resolve](/guide/incident/overview) | Investigates incidents and finds the root cause with evidence |
    | [Review](/guide/code-review/overview) | Reviews pull requests with inline comments and suggested fixes |
    | [Cyber](/guide/security/cyber-overview) | Pentests an app you're authorized to test and tracks each finding |
    | [Optimize](/guide/cost-optimization/overview) | Ranks idle resources, oversized instances, and cost anomalies |

    Two more products shape and run those agents rather than investigate:

    | Product | What it does |
    |---|---|
    | [Customize](/guide/agents/customize) | Shapes how agents work: custom agents, skills, knowledge, approvals |
    | [Automation](/guide/automation/automations) | Runs a prompt on a schedule, when a webhook fires, on a repository event, or when a probe check fires |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How fast can I get a first answer?">
    About 10 minutes. The [Quickstart](/quickstart) walks you through signing in at [app.cloudthinker.io](https://app.cloudthinker.io), connecting one system read-only, and asking your first question.

    **Success state:** the answer names services, regions, and resources you recognize from your environment.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I try it without risking production?">
    Yes. Connect a staging or sandbox account with a read-only role, and turn off **Allow writes** on the connection so CloudThinker refuses any change. Agents can look but can't change anything. See [Quickstart](/quickstart).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between an organization and a workspace?">
    The **organization** is your account: plan, billing, seats, the member list, SSO, BYOK, and audit logs. Its roles are Owner, Admin, Developer, and Viewer. A **workspace** sits inside it and holds the actual work: connections, agents, skills, conversations, and approvals. Its roles are Admin, Developer, and Viewer.

    | Settings area | Covers | Open it from |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Admin Settings** | The organization (Owners and Admins only) | Your avatar at the bottom of the sidebar, then **Admin settings** |
    | **Workspace settings** | The workspace you're in | **More → Workspace settings** in the sidebar |
    | **Account Settings** | Just you: profile, 2FA, your own email notifications | Your avatar menu |

    See [Organizations](/guide/organization) and [Workspaces](/guide/workspaces).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I invite my team?">
    An organization Owner or Admin goes to **Admin Settings → Organization** and clicks **Invite Members**. Each person gets an organization role, and you can give them a different role in a single workspace from **More → Workspace settings → Members**. See [invite members](/guide/organization#invite-members) and [Workspace members](/guide/workspace-users).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which systems can CloudThinker connect to?">
    Clouds such as AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure, plus Kubernetes, databases, observability tools, Git providers, CI/CD, and ticketing tools such as Jira, ServiceNow, and PagerDuty. Each one is a **connection**: a credential for one service, added in **More → Workspace settings → Connections**.

    The full list is in the [connection catalog](/guide/connections/overview#connection-catalog). If a tool isn't listed but you run an MCP server in front of it, add that server as an [MCP connection](/guide/connections/mcp) and agents can call its tools.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to install anything or change my architecture?">
    No. CloudThinker runs as a managed service and reaches your systems through the connections you add, using the credentials and permissions you grant.

    If you want commands to run on a machine you control, such as a laptop or CI runner, you can add a [Worker](/guide/workers) (beta). It's optional. Use one when the agent needs a private repository or an internal host: the Worker connects outward over HTTPS, so you open no inbound port.

    These docs don't publish CloudThinker's egress IP addresses. If your firewall needs an allowlist, ask through [contact us](https://cloudthinker.io/contact).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I use CloudThinker from Slack or Teams?">
    An organization Owner or Admin goes to **Admin Settings → Integrations**, opens the **Slack** card, and clicks **Connect with Slack**. Then invite the bot to a channel. Chat apps need the Team plan or above. In Slack and Microsoft Teams, a write that needs approval links back to the web app, where you approve it. Google Chat shows **Approve** and **Reject** buttons on the card itself. See [Slack](/guide/slack-integration), [Microsoft Teams](/guide/teams-integration), and [Google Chat](/guide/google-chat-integration).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I remove a connection or delete our data?">
    A workspace Admin can delete a connection from **More → Workspace settings → Connections**. To remove a whole workspace, including its connections, conversations, and settings, an organization Owner or Admin uses **Admin Settings → Workspaces**. There's no restore. See [Delete a workspace](/guide/workspaces#delete-a-workspace).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Agents and safety

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Who are the agents?">
    Every workspace has one built-in agent, **CloudThinker**. It's always on, and it can split a big task across temporary subagents. Your team can also create custom agents with their own instructions and skills. See [Agents](/guide/agents/overview) and [Customize](/guide/agents/customize).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to learn special commands?">
    No. Plain language works, and with no mention, CloudThinker answers. Three optional symbols make a request more precise:

    | Symbol | Picks | Example |
    |---|---|---|
    | `@` | An agent | `@CloudThinker` |
    | `#` | A connection or knowledge base | `#aws-prod` |
    | `/` | A skill or command | `/resource-rightsizing-analysis` |

    ```text
    Which EC2 instances in us-east-1 averaged under 5% CPU last week?
    @CloudThinker #aws-prod which RDS instances averaged over 80% CPU yesterday?
    Show last month's cost by service as a dashboard
    ```

    Ask for output shapes such as a dashboard or report in words. Full syntax: [Commands](/guide/commands).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can agents change my production systems on their own?">
    Only within limits you set. Reads always run. Every **write** (an agent call that changes a connected system, such as restarting a service) passes these checks in order:

    1. **Always blocked**: a built-in list of calls that never run, such as stopping CloudTrail logging or deleting the `kube-system` namespace. Nothing overrides it.
    2. Your **Rules**, which allow, ask first, or deny a named kind of write. Under **Manual**, an Allow Rule doesn't let a write run; the write still goes to a person.
    3. The **Approval mode**: **Manual** asks a person for every write. **Auto** can let AI judgment allow a safe write, then asks a person or refuses the rest.
    4. An approver, who approves or declines.

    One exception: in Review, **Apply fix** and **Fix all findings** commit to the pull request's source branch when a person clicks them, without going through these checks.

    New workspaces start on **Auto · Rules + AI · Ask me**. Read it in three parts: the mode is **Auto**, writes are decided by your **Rules + AI** judgment, and anything still not allowed waits for a person (**Ask me**). For example, restarting a staging pod you just asked about can run. Deleting an RDS snapshot is destructive, so it asks.

    When a write waits, the chat shows an approval card with **Approve** and **Decline**. By default any workspace Developer or Admin can answer it. Narrow that in [Approvers](/guide/approvals/approvers). A request from Slack or Microsoft Teams links back to the web app, where you approve it. In Google Chat you can approve or reject it on the card.

    To require a person for every write, switch the workspace to **Manual**. Then, under [Run types](/guide/approvals/approval-mode#set-a-mode-for-a-run-type), override **Automations** (and any other run type you've changed) to **Manual** too: the Automations row defaults to **Auto · Rules + AI · Refuse** and doesn't follow the workspace. An Automation or a chat can also pick its own mode. See [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview) and [Approval mode](/guide/approvals/approval-mode).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What permissions does CloudThinker need in my cloud?">
    Read-only is enough to start. Two controls set what agents can do:

    | Control | Where | What it decides |
    |---|---|---|
    | The credential's own permissions | Your provider (IAM role, service account, API token scopes) | The ceiling. Agents never do more than it allows. |
    | **Allow writes** | The connection's detail panel | Off: every change is refused. On: changes go through Rules and the Approval mode. |

    Recommended read-only starting roles:

    | Cloud | Role | Setup |
    |---|---|---|
    | AWS | The managed `ReadOnlyAccess` policy, created by the setup script you run in CloudShell | [AWS setup](/guide/connections/aws#setup) |
    | Google Cloud | **Viewer** (`roles/viewer`). For Optimize, add **BigQuery Data Viewer** and **BigQuery Job User**. | [Google Cloud](/guide/connections/gcp) |
    | Azure | **Reader** on the subscription, plus **Cost Management Reader** for spend | [Azure](/guide/connections/azure) |

    **Allow writes** is on by default for AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure, so the read-only credential is what blocks changes. To block them in CloudThinker too, turn the switch off on the connection's detail panel. Add write permissions only for specific changes you want agents to make. See [Connections](/guide/connections/overview).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Security and compliance

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What data does CloudThinker store, and how is it protected?">
    | Data | How it's protected |
    |---|---|
    | Connection credentials | Encrypted at rest. Injected into the managed sandbox when a command runs, never sent to a Worker. |
    | Conversations, findings, reports | Scoped to the workspace and visible by role. Known secrets (connection credentials, workspace credentials, and values saved from chat) are replaced with placeholders before they're stored. |
    | Audit logs | Kept 90 days, then deleted automatically. Export to CSV to keep them longer |
    | Account passwords | Stored as bcrypt hashes |

    The app and API are served over HTTPS. Model calls go to CloudThinker's LLM provider unless your organization uses [BYOK](/guide/byok) (bring your own LLM credentials), which routes them through your own AWS Bedrock, Anthropic, or FPT Cloud key.

    These docs don't cover the hosting region, the model provider's name and data terms, training use, or conversation retention. Ask for them through [contact us](https://cloudthinker.io/contact) when you start a vendor review. Details: [Security and trust](/guide/security/overview).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if someone pastes a secret into chat?">
    The message doesn't send. CloudThinker scans it for credential-shaped values such as API keys, tokens, passwords, and connection strings, and opens the **Review detected secrets** dialog. Pick one:

    | Button | Result |
    |---|---|
    | **Redact from message** | Replaces each value with `[sensitive data removed]` |
    | **Save secrets** (workspace Admin only) | Saves each value as a workspace credential and rewrites the message to say `$KEY` |

    Mark a false positive with **Mark as not a secret**. The raw value never enters the conversation. See [Data protection](/guide/security/data-protection).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we use SSO, enforce 2FA, and keep an audit trail?">
    | Control | Where | Plans |
    |---|---|---|
    | Authenticator app (TOTP) | **Account Settings → 2FA** | All |
    | SSO with SAML 2.0 or OIDC, SCIM sync | **Admin Settings → Identity and access** | Scale, Scale Plus, Enterprise |
    | Audit logs, kept 90 days, CSV export | **Admin Settings → Audit Logs** | Scale, Scale Plus, Enterprise |

    See [Single sign-on](/guide/security/sso), [Roles](/guide/security/roles), and [Audit logs](/guide/security/audit-logs).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does CloudThinker hold compliance certifications?">
    Certifications and attestations aren't published in these docs. For current reports, a security questionnaire, or a data processing agreement, [contact us](https://cloudthinker.io/contact) and the team will send what's available. To answer the rest of a vendor review now, use [Security and trust](/guide/security/overview).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we run CloudThinker inside our own environment?">
    By default it runs as a managed service at `app.cloudthinker.io`. Dedicated, self-hosted, and air-gapped deployments are in early access through a design-partner program. See [Deployment](/guide/deployment/overview).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Pricing and plans

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How is CloudThinker priced?">
    Per seat (one seat per member). Each paid plan adds a monthly pool of **credits**, the unit agent work consumes, shared by the whole organization. Plans are Free, Team, Scale, Scale Plus, and Enterprise. Yearly billing saves 20% on the seat price.

    | Plan | Monthly price per seat | Premium credits per seat |
    |---|---|---|
    | Free | Free | None |
    | Team | $25 | 200 |
    | Scale | $100 | 1,000 |
    | Scale Plus | $200 | 2,000 |
    | Enterprise | Custom | Custom |

    Every plan also gets 100 daily free credits for chat on the Light model. They reset at midnight UTC.

    Each run uses one of three model tiers, which multiply its credit charge: **Light** (0.3x) for quick questions, **Pro** (1.0x, the default) for everyday work, and **Ultra** (1.7x) for the hardest problems.

    When your credits run out, runs that need more credits are blocked. On Team and above, the Owner can turn on [Pay-As-You-Go](/guide/billing/usage#pay-as-you-go): a metered top-up at $0.10 per credit, up to a monthly cap you set. Current prices and limits: [Pricing](/guide/billing/pricing). How runs spend credits: [Usage](/guide/billing/usage).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What can I do on the Free plan?">
    Direct chat on the Light model: web chat, the [CLI](/guide/cli/overview), and Rooms (chats shared with other people). Any other run, such as Review, Resolve, Optimize, Cyber, or an Automation, is refused with "The Free plan only supports direct chat." Upgrade to Team or above to unlock them and the Pro and Ultra models.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a free trial?">
    Eligible Free organizations see **Start 14-Day Free Trial** on the Team card in **Admin Settings → Billing → Upgrade Plan**. You get Team features for 14 days. Pay-As-You-Go (paid top-up credits once your pool runs out) stays off during the trial.

    To keep Team, the Owner opens **Admin Settings → Billing → Upgrade Plan** and clicks **Add payment method** on the Team card.

    <Warning>
      If you don't add a payment method before the trial ends, the organization returns to Free. Runs other than direct chat stop, and Pro and Ultra models lock.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which plan do I need?">
    | You want to | Lowest plan |
    |---|---|
    | Try CloudThinker in chat at no cost | Free |
    | Run Review, Resolve, Optimize, or Cyber, or answer from Slack, Teams, or Google Chat | Team |
    | Use SSO, keep an audit trail, use BYOK, or cap spend per workspace | Scale |
    | Same as Scale with twice the premium credits per seat (2,000 instead of 1,000) and up to 10 workspaces instead of 5 | Scale Plus |
    | Run CloudThinker on your own infrastructure | Enterprise, through the early-access design-partner program |

    Only the organization **Owner** can change the plan. See [Pricing](/guide/billing/pricing).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
