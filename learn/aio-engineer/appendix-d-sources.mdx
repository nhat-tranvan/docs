Every figure in this edition comes from one of the primary sources below. Use this page to check a number before you repeat it, and to decide how far it should move your own evaluation.

## How to read the numbers

Each number in the book belongs to one class. The class tells you how much to trust it.

| Class | Example in this book | How to weigh it |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent benchmark** | ITBench, ITBench-AA | Strongest public signal for models. It measures a model under the benchmark's harness, not a platform under yours. |
| **Research paper or preprint** | SREGym, AIOpsLab | Good for failure modes and method. Check the setup before you reuse a percentage. |
| **Vendor first-party** | Microsoft's Azure SRE Agent posts, Anthropic's multi-agent write-up | Useful engineering lessons. Treat outcome numbers as the vendor's own claim. |
| **Standard or specification** | OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions, Model Context Protocol | Defines terms and shared schemas. Not evidence that any product works. |

Four rules apply to every figure:

1. **Provenance is stated.** The chapter names the source and its class next to the number. If a figure has no source, treat it as a guess.
2. **Ranges beat points.** "About 69–88%" is more honest than one precise-looking number. Be wary of a single decimal-point figure with no method behind it.
3. **Your baseline beats every benchmark.** A public score tells you where the field is. Your own incidents, replayed under your own harness, tell you whether a platform works for you.
4. **Scores don't transfer.** A benchmark scores a model under its harness. Your proof of concept scores a platform under yours. Neither predicts the other, which is why [Appendix A](/learn/aio-engineer/appendix-a-poc-test-plan) exists.

## Check a figure before you use it

Run these checks before a number goes into a business case or a vendor scorecard.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the class">
    Match the figure to its row above. A vendor first-party number needs more checking than an independent benchmark.

    **Success state:** you can name the source and its class in one line, for example "ITBench, independent benchmark, ICML 2025".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the harness">
    Note what the score measured: which model, which tools, which scenarios, and what counted as success. SREGym shows that "the alert cleared" and "the defect is fixed" are different outcomes.

    **Success state:** you know whether the figure measures detection, diagnosis, or a verified fix.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Replace it with your own number">
    Replay 10 or more of your own incidents through the platform using the golden set from [Appendix A](/learn/aio-engineer/appendix-a-poc-test-plan). Score them with the [Chapter 7](/learn/aio-engineer/chapter-7-evaluating-agents) rubric.

    **Success state:** your business case cites your own replay scores, with public benchmarks only as context.
  </Step>
</Steps>
