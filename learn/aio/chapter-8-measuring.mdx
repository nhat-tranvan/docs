*Agentic operations succeeds or fails on evidence. Instrument the program like the production system it is.*

This chapter gives you the numbers to track, the business case to build from them, and the test harness that tells you whether an agent is safe to trust. Section 8.6 shows where CloudThinker reports each number.

## 8.1 What improves, and in what order

Gains arrive in a predictable order. Plan your reporting around it, so nobody expects autonomous fixes in week two.

| Order | Metric | Where the gain comes from |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| First | Alert volume | Correlation, deduplication, and symptom suppression before a person is paged |
| Second | Time to root cause | Agents gather logs, metrics, and change history in parallel, which shortens diagnosis, the longest phase of most incidents |
| Second | Toil | Triage, evidence gathering, and reporting move from L1/L2 engineers to agents |
| Ongoing | Cloud cost | Continuous rightsizing and idle-resource cleanup instead of a quarterly review |
| Last | Time to resolve (MTTR) | Pre-approved fixes for the action classes you've already proven safe |

How large each gain gets depends on your data quality and how far you've climbed the trust ladder ([§7.3](/learn/aio/chapter-7-humans-on-the-loop)).

The strongest published figures are vendor numbers. [AWS reports](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/announcing-general-availability-of-aws-devops-agent/) DevOps Agent preview customers seeing up to 75% lower MTTR, 80% faster investigations, and 94% root-cause accuracy. In the same announcement, Western Governors University (WGU) describes an estimated two-hour resolution cut to 28 minutes.

Treat these as claims to test in your own pilot, not results to plan around.

## 8.2 The operating dashboard

Run the program on eight KPIs. Review them monthly with the on-call rotation.

| KPI | What it tells you | How to read it |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **MTTD and MTTR** | Time to detect, and mean time to resolve (from incident start to resolution) | Trend by severity. Keep agent-handled and human-handled incidents separate, or the averages hide the change. |
| **Autonomous resolution rate** | Share of incidents resolved with no human action | The best single maturity indicator. It should climb as action classes graduate. |
| **Recommendation acceptance rate** | Share of agent proposals approved without changes | Three bars: below about 70%, tune the agent's context or your policy. At 80% overall, the Approve phase of the pilot can exit ([§9.2](/learn/aio/chapter-9-roadmap)). Above about 95% for one action class, the approval step adds little, so graduate that class. |
| **Rollback and intervention rate** | Agent actions that had to be reversed or overridden | The safety counterweight. If it rises, slow autonomy down. |
| **Repeat-incident rate** | Whether the same problems keep coming back | Falling repeats mean memory and problem management are working. |
| **Coverage ratio** | Share of incident classes the agents can handle at L2 or above | Shows where to write the next runbook or add the next connection. |
| **On-call load** | Pages per engineer per week, after-hours pages separately | The metric your engineers and their managers feel most. |
| **Agent unit cost** | Model and platform spend per incident resolved and per service covered | Track it from day one. See §8.4. |

The autonomy levels (L0 to L4) are defined in [§3.2](/learn/aio/chapter-3-what-is-aio).

## 8.3 Building the business case

The return side has three lines. Use your own inputs for each.

| Line | How to calculate it | Input to get |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Downtime avoided** | Incidents per year × cost per minute of downtime × minutes of MTTR reduction you measured in the pilot | Cost per minute from your finance team. Without one, use the [Splunk and Oxford Economics 2026 benchmark](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m05/the-600-billion-wake-up-call-new-splunk-research-reveals-downtime-is-a-systemic-business-crisis.html) of about $15,000 per minute for Global 2000 companies. It runs higher for payment and trading systems. |
| **Toil converted** | Hours of L1/L2 work agents absorb × loaded engineering cost per hour | Hours from your ticket system or a two-week time study. Often the largest line where engineers are hard to hire. |
| **Cloud waste recovered** | Verified monthly savings from implemented cost recommendations × 12 | Your cloud bill before and after each change, not the estimate shown when the recommendation was made |

The cost side covers the platform subscription, model usage, and the engineering time to integrate and govern the system. Include the autonomy-policy owner's time.

Test the case with one rule: if it only works with the downtime line, you picked the wrong pilot domain. Choose a domain where toil savings carry the case, and treat avoided downtime as upside.

## 8.4 The unit economics of an agent

Most of an agentic program's running cost is model inference. Most inference cost depends on where the expensive reasoning runs. So keep cheap perception always on, and save frontier reasoning for the few signals that need it.

[§4.3](/learn/aio/chapter-4-architecture) calls this two-tier sensing. Read one incident as a cost object:

| Tier | What runs | How often | Cost per run |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Sensing | Lightweight perception over every signal | Always | Lowest |
| Triage | Grouping and filtering of signals worth a look | On every signal that isn't plain noise | Higher |
| Deep resolution | Multi-step reasoning: root cause, fix, validation | Only on signals that survive triage | Highest by far |

Deep resolution costs the most per run but fires rarely, so the expensive tier stays mostly idle while every signal is still covered. We don't publish typical ratios, because they vary too much between environments. Measure three numbers in your own: signals per day, the share that escalates to deep resolution, and the cost of one deep run. Multiply them to get your daily deep-resolution spend.

That's why cost per resolved incident and cost per covered service are on the dashboard. An architecture that runs frontier reasoning on every noisy signal erases its own return before the first renewal. You won't see it happen unless you track the per-incident number from the start.

When you compare vendors, ask how pricing tracks value. An outcome-based option, such as a fee tied to verified savings, removes the vendor's incentive to run up inference for its own sake. Whatever the structure, the figure in your business case should be the one you measured against your own 90-day baseline ([§9.2](/learn/aio/chapter-9-roadmap)).

## 8.5 Testing an agent before you trust it

Earlier chapters describe agentic operations as self-verifying. This section shows what that check looks like, and how to catch a confidently wrong fix before it reaches production.

An evaluation harness has four parts:

| Part | What it does |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Scenario library** | Recorded real incidents plus deliberately injected faults, each with a known correct outcome: the right root cause, the safe action, the clean rollback |
| **Shadow run** | The agent acts against a sandbox copy of production, never production itself, so you can watch its proposed actions without consequence |
| **Scoring against ground truth** | Did it find the right root cause? Did it pick a safe action? Would its rollback have worked? |
| **Regression gate** | A new agent version, prompt, or skill must pass the library before it ships, like any other production code |

Agents aren't deterministic. The same scenario can produce different behavior on different runs. So run each scenario many times and score the spread of outcomes. A fix that's right 70% of the time is a different risk from one that's right 99% of the time, and only repeated runs show which one you have.

A concrete check, for a database connection-exhaustion scenario. The harness asserts that the agent:

1. Names connection-pool exhaustion as the root cause.
2. Restores the correct connection limit, instead of only restarting the service.
3. Confirms the connection count returns to baseline and stays there.

Step 3 is the same Validate step the agent runs in production, tested here against a known answer. If an agent can't pass its own validation check in the harness, don't let it run unattended in production.

**In CloudThinker:** you can replay a closed incident by asking for an investigation of that time window with the workspace on **Manual**, as in [§7.3](/learn/aio/chapter-7-humans-on-the-loop), and compare the answer to your postmortem. These docs don't describe a built-in scenario library, sandbox shadow run, or regression gate, so plan to build those yourself.

## 8.6 Where to read these numbers in CloudThinker

If you're running a pilot on CloudThinker, these pages report most of the dashboard. Track on-call load and rollback rate in your paging and change tools.

| KPI | Where to find it |
| :---- | :---- |
| MTTD | Not reported as its own number. **Where the time goes** shows the **Detect** stage, and your monitoring tool has the rest. |
| MTTR and its trend | **Resolve → Insights → Overview**: **Mean time to resolve** and **MTTR over time** ([Insights](/guide/incident/insights)) |
| Time to root cause | **Resolve → Insights → Overview**: **Alert → root cause**, plus **Where the time goes** for each stage |
| Autonomous resolution rate | **Resolve → Insights → Overview**: **Auto-resolved**, and **Manual takeover** per service |
| Recommendation acceptance | **Resolve → Insights → Accuracy & trust**: **Fix acceptance** and **Root-cause accuracy**, both based on your team's reviews |
| Repeat incidents | **Resolve → Insights → Overview**: **Recurrence trend** |
| Alert noise (supporting metric for MTTD and on-call load) | **Resolve → Insights → Pulse analysis**: **Noise suppressed** and **Signals ingested** ([Pulse analytics](/guide/pulse/analytics)) |
| Coverage ratio | **Resolve → Insights → Runbook analytics**: **Runbook coverage** and **Coverage gaps** |
| Cloud waste | **Optimize → Findings** shows estimates for open findings, not realized savings ([how savings are counted](/guide/cost-optimization/savings)). Mark a finding **Implemented** when the change ships, then verify the saving against your cloud bill. |
| Agent unit cost | **Admin Settings → Usage** shows credits **By Feature**, **By User**, and **By Workspace** ([usage](/guide/billing/usage)), not per incident. For cost per resolved incident, divide Resolve's credits for the month by resolved incidents in Insights for the same month. Convert credits to money with your plan's [pricing](/guide/billing/pricing). |

**Root-cause accuracy** only means something once your team reviews enough incidents. Check **Your review coverage** on the same tab before you report it.

<Tip>
**Baseline before you deploy.** The most common business-case failure is having no credible "before". Insights only covers incidents CloudThinker has handled, so pull the last 90 days of MTTR, alert volume, and page counts from your paging and ticket tools, and toil hours from a time study, before the first agent touches production.
</Tip>
