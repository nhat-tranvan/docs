*Agentic operations is a people change wearing a technology costume.*

This chapter covers the shift from doing operations work to supervising it, how each operations role changes, and the four-step trust ladder for handing work to agents. Section 7.5 maps each step of the ladder to CloudThinker settings.

## 7.1 From executor to supervisor

"Human in the loop" means a person sits inside the execution path: every step waits for them. "Human on the loop" means a person supervises: they set intent and policy, approve consequential actions, audit outcomes, and step in by exception.

| | Human in the loop | Human on the loop |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Who runs each step | The engineer | The agent |
| What the engineer does | Triage, run commands, write the fix | Set policy, approve high-impact writes, review outcomes |
| When the engineer is needed | Every step | Novel failures and consequential decisions |
| What limits throughput | Engineer hours | How much autonomy policy allows |

Think of a pilot hand-flying a plane versus a pilot managing the autopilot. The second pilot isn't less important. They're responsible for more of the aircraft with less manual work, and they take the controls when judgment matters most.

Teams that do well redesign roles on purpose. Teams that don't end up with engineers who rubber-stamp approvals they no longer understand.

## 7.2 How roles change

| Role today | What shrinks | What grows |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| SRE or on-call engineer | Manual triage, digging through logs, 3 a.m. mechanical fixes | Policy design, agent supervision, engineering for novel failures, reliability architecture |
| DevOps or platform engineer | Ticket-driven provisioning, repeated pipeline fixes | Agent enablement: connections, context and runbooks, golden paths, evaluation |
| Ops manager or IT operations leader | Headcount-based capacity planning, war-room coordination | Autonomy governance, managing which work agents own, outcome-based vendor management |
| Security engineer | Manual hunts for misconfigurations, collecting compliance screenshots | Guardrail design, agent permission design, continuous compliance checks |

Two new jobs appear. Treat both as career paths, not side duties.

- **Agent operations engineer.** Owns the health of the agents themselves: instructions, connections, skills, memory, evaluations, and cost.
- **Autonomy policy owner.** Often a senior SRE or engineering manager. Decides which kinds of action move up the trust ladder, and settles disputes when an agent and a person disagree.

## 7.3 Trust is built in increments

Engineers come to trust an agent in a predictable order. Skipping a step backfires.

| Step | What the agent does | What people do | Move on when |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1. Watch it investigate** | Investigates and recommends. Makes no changes. | Compare the agent's root-cause analysis against their own. | At least 70% of its root-cause analyses are rated right by on-call. |
| **2. Approve its actions** | Proposes a complete fix and waits. | Approve or reject each write. Every decision is a labeled data point on the agent's judgment. | At least 80% of its proposals are accepted, with zero harmful actions. Graduate one action class at a time once it's about 95% approved without changes, with no rollbacks ([§8.2](/learn/aio/chapter-8-measuring)). |
| **3. Pre-approve the routine** | Runs those proven actions alone. | Get notified, and review the record afterward. | The rollback and intervention rate for the whole domain stays flat or falls. |
| **4. Delegate a domain** | Owns a bounded domain end to end, such as idle-resource cleanup, cache management, or certificate rotation. | Audit outcomes on a schedule instead of approving each action. | Final step. Re-review it after any incident and at each scheduled audit. |

The 70% and 80% bars match the pilot exit criteria in [§9.2](/learn/aio/chapter-9-roadmap). In CloudThinker, on-call rates each root-cause analysis under **Was Resolve's diagnosis right?** on the incident, and only the workspace Admin role can record that rating. Give your graders the Admin role, or have an Admin record on-call's verdicts.

The failure mode is consistent: skip steps and you ship an agent that is confidently wrong at 3 a.m. One such incident can set an agentic program back by months. Take the steps in order and autonomy compounds.

A practical test for step 1: give the agent an incident your team has already closed, and compare answers.

```text
Investigate why checkout-api returned 5xx errors between 14:00 and 14:20 UTC yesterday. Give the root cause, the evidence for it, and the fix you would propose. Don't change anything.
```

Replay several closed incidents this way. If it gets the root cause right on at least 70% of them, with evidence that matches your postmortems, move to step 2.

## 7.4 Measure the time you get back

The payoff of the supervisor model is senior time moved from toil to prevention work. Measure it, or it won't show up in the business case:

- **On-call load:** pages per engineer per week, with after-hours pages counted separately, from your paging tool ([§8.2](/learn/aio/chapter-8-measuring)).
- **Toil hours:** hours of triage and evidence gathering per week, from your ticket system or a two-week time study ([§8.3](/learn/aio/chapter-8-measuring)).

Capture both before the pilot and again at each step of the ladder.

## 7.5 The trust ladder in CloudThinker

In CloudThinker, reads always run and every write passes through [Approvals](/guide/approvals/overview). Each step of the ladder is a setting you can see and change.

| Step | Approval mode | How to set it up | Where |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1. Watch it investigate** | **Manual** | Set **Workspace mode** to **Manual** before you connect anything, so every write waits for a person. If you'll run Automations, click **Override** on the **Automations** row under **Run types** and pick **Manual** too, since that row doesn't follow the workspace. A chat can also pick its own mode in the composer, so leave it on the workspace mode. Ask for investigations and say "don't change anything". | **Approvals** in the sidebar, **Approval mode** tab |
| **2. Approve its actions** | Stay on **Manual** | Answer each approval card in chat or in [Google Chat](/guide/google-chat-integration). [Slack](/guide/slack-integration) and [Microsoft Teams](/guide/teams-integration) link back to the web app to answer it. Turn on **Agent approvals** in [Notifications](/guide/notifications) to get requests by email or chat. Choose who may answer with [Approvers](/guide/approvals/approvers) groups, and turn on the two-person rule for the riskiest writes. | **Approvals** in the sidebar, **Approvers** tab |
| **3. Pre-approve the routine** | Switch to **Auto · Rules · Ask me** | Accept an **Allow** [Rule](/guide/approvals/rules) for one operation on one connection that cleared the step 2 bar. Agents propose Rules as they work; on the **Rules** tab, click **Find rules** to get more. Proposals wait on the **Needs review** tab, and a banner with **Review** shows when any are waiting. Nothing changes until an admin accepts. Then switch the mode: Allow Rules run writes alone only under **Auto**, and only once CloudThinker turns on [Rule enforcement](/guide/approvals/rules#troubleshooting) for your workspace. Until it's on, stay on **Manual**, which holds every write with a person today. With AI judgment off, every write no Rule allows still asks a person. | **Approvals** in the sidebar, **Rules** and **Approval mode** tabs |
| **4. Delegate a domain** | **Auto · Rules + AI · Refuse** for the Automation | Run the domain as an [Automation](/guide/automation/automations). Accept an **Allow** Rule for each routine write, scoped to **Automations** under **Runs from**. A write no Rule allows and AI judgment doesn't pass is refused instead of waiting for someone who isn't there. Each week, open **Activity**, check the **Refused** counts and the **No Rule matched** row, and add a Rule for any routine write that was refused. | **Approvals** in the sidebar, **Activity** tab |

New workspaces start on **Auto · Rules + AI · Ask me**. There, accepted Rules run, and AI judgment can also let a write no Rule covers run by itself when it judges the write safe and within what you asked. That puts the default past step 3 for those writes, so switch to **Manual** for step 1. Move to **Auto · Rules + AI · Ask me** only once your team trusts the agent's judgment on your systems.
